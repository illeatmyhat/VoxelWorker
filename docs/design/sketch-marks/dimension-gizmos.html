Dimension gizmos

Spans, radii and angles, drawn in the viewport at true scale. Terminators are always filled arrowheads, 3:1, screen-stable. Ink is not red — it is the theme's high-contrast foreground, carried over any ground by a halo. A non-driving dimension is parenthesised whole: (R21), not R (21).

Ink and ground — the dark-theme-plus-bright-blocks problem

The halo is the answer to the ground problem. Over the dark viewport it is invisible; over a bright block it is the only thing keeping the dimension readable. It costs one extra pass and no colour decision, and it inverts with the theme for free — so no dimension colour has to be chosen per material.
Every halo is painted before any ink, as two passes over the whole gizmo — not halo-then-ink per element, which is what let an arrowhead's halo bite into the dimension line it terminates. Layer order is: geometry, all halos, all ink, then values.
The value is the one exception, and deliberately: its halo is painted over the dimension line, because a dimension line is supposed to break where the number sits.

Radius — the leader is radial by construction

The arc point is derived from the anchor, never stored beside it. a = atan2(anchor − centre), then the arc point is centre + r·(cos a, sin a) and the jog is the anchor projected onto that same ray. A radius is only a radius because the leader points at the centre; deriving it means no anchor can break that. Same discipline as ADR 0008 and the anchored-DDA law — carry the frame, never re-derive a value that must agree with another.
Text rides the leader at its angle, above it, flipped 180° whenever it would otherwise read upside-down.
