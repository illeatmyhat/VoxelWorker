Constraint marks

Constraints are red and white; tools are blue and white. No plate, no badge — the two-tone glyph is the category. Drawn on a 36-unit canvas, not necessarily square: each mark gets the box its own shape wants.
Two-tone rule, taken from Fusion's own constraint icons: red is the driven entity (the first selection, the thing that moves), white is the reference it is driven against.

Mirror and the patterns are not constraints. They generate sketch entities, so they are blue tools and live with the tool set — see the second table. This settles two collisions that had no other cheap fix: Flip vs Symmetry, and Snap-to-voxel vs Quantize. Both pairs are near-identical constructions, and both are now separated by the colour family rather than by redrawing one of them into something worse. Hue is doing real work a plate could not: it says which kind of thing this is before the shape is read.

Quantize is not a smooth equation, and that is an architectural problem, not a glyph one. "This value is an integer multiple of a voxel" cannot be handed to the Dog Leg solver as a residual — there is no continuous derivative to descend. It has to be a projection applied after the solve, or an integer the solver holds fixed between edits. Worth settling before slice E grows its first caller, because it decides whether Constraint is one trait or two.
Collinear only exists because we are Fusion-shaped. Onshape deletes it — its Coincident acts on the infinite underlying geometry, so coincident-on-two-lines is collinear. Keeping it is right for transfer, but it is a choice, and it is why Collinear and Coincident have to look unrelated.
