Sketch tools and modifiers — resolved set

33 marks, each at 36 px and 16 px. The ruling: Fusion's compositions (pass B) are the house style, six marks take pass A instead, seven are redrawn as D to stay clear of Autodesk's authored expression, and Construction gets a third ink. The four passes that produced it are kept below as provenance.

The ruling, in full. B is the default and needs no per-mark justification — Autodesk's compositions are better than mine, and the four claims of theirs I could have got wrong were checked against the shipped 16×16 PNGs and all four held: Line really does run into a tangent arc, Point really is monochrome #666666, Trim really is scissors with the blade crossing the cut line, and the polygons really are pentagons.
Six marks take A, listed with reasons below. Four of the six are cases where B is not merely different but wrong — a datum symbol, a non-similar pair of rectangles, a pictogram that names the neighbouring tool, and a discriminator Fusion never needed.
Construction takes a third ink, orange, dashed. This is a depiction, not a code: construction geometry already renders orange-dashed in the viewport, so the mark quotes the linetype instead of inventing one. It does not generalise — no other tool owns a viewport ink.
Polyline and Close loop are cut. Neither earns a rail slot.
D — clear of their art. Seven marks are redrawn so the set does not ship Autodesk's expression. The test is not "does this resemble Fusion" but was Autodesk's choice forced or arbitrary. A fillet is a rounded corner and a 3-tangent circle is the incircle of a triangle — there is essentially one way to draw either, so the drawing carries no authorship and copying it takes nothing. But nothing about trim requires scissors, nothing about rectangle requires breaking the outline at the picked corners, and nothing about construction requires a wedge. Those are authored, and those are the ones redrawn.
Each D keeps B's concept — what the icon is about, and Fusion's picks-versus-result polarity — and changes the drawing. Four of the seven came out better than the thing they replace, which is the useful part: Conic stops colliding with Three-point arc, Circle stops reading as a prohibition sign, Offset starts telling the truth about convex corners, and Break stops depicting a removal it does not perform.
Engineering judgement, not legal advice — if this set ever ships commercially, the twenty-minute version of this question is worth putting to someone qualified.

Provenance — the four passes

What the ruling was made from. Two inks throughout: white line art and one blue accent. What changes between the passes is what the accent is for.

A — accent is the output. Blue is what the tool makes or does; white is what you hand it. My first pass, arrived at before reading Autodesk's icons.
B — accent is Fusion's. Autodesk's own composition and accent semantics, ported to two inks. Measured off the shipped 16×16 PNGs, not from prose — no Autodesk page describes an icon in words. Fusion's Create panel puts the accent on the picks; its Modify panel puts it on the result.
C — accent is the difference. One rule that derives both of Fusion's behaviours: blue marks only what separates this tool from its nearest sibling, drawn to dominate. Five circle tools make the same circle, so the picks are the difference; Fillet and Chamfer cut the same corner, so the new edge is. A tool with no sibling gets no blue at all — which is what Fusion does for Point and Text. I originally counted Move/Copy as a third confirmation; it is not one. Move/Copy is monochrome because it is a shared cross-workspace glyph, so citing it as evidence for the rule was circular.
Corrections the research forced, applied in B and C: Fusion draws no slot centreline, no spline control polygon, no conic chord or rho axis, and no ellipse axis ticks — I invented all four in A. Its polygons are pentagons, which read as "n-sided" where a hexagon reads as "hexagon". Its dashes are reserved for a linetype (construction, in a third colour), never for a state, so A's dashed-means-doomed and dashed-means-pending both borrow a channel Fusion spends elsewhere.
Filed wrong on my sheet: Mirror and both patterns are in Fusion's Create panel, not Modify. Since confirmed: sketch Fillet does emit Tangent constraints — it was my inference, and it is now fact rather than a hedge.
Re-verified against the shipped PNGs after all four were disputed from memory: Line is a segment running into a right-bulging arc with three #178FE6 2×2 picks; Point is one colour, #666666, with no blue anywhere; Trim is charcoal #333333 scissors whose vertical blade crosses the cut line on its own row; all three polygons are pentagons — 2 px apex, 10–12 px flat bottom, impossible for a hexagon in any orientation. Every disputed claim held.
